What makes a key high-security

A patented keyway is a promise that nobody copies your key by accident.

A standard house key is an open design. Anyone with a blank and a grinder can copy it in two minutes. A high-security key is built differently. The keyway is patented, the blade profile is machined to tighter tolerances, and many systems add a second layer inside the cylinder: sidebars, rotating discs, or a moving element in the key itself that has to line up before the plug will turn.

The other half of the protection is paperwork. Blanks for these systems are not sold to hardware stores or vending kiosks. They are distributed under control, and restricted systems are tied to a key control card or an authorization on file that names who is allowed to order keys. That is the part people find out about the hard way, usually on a Friday afternoon with one key left on the ring.

We deal with both halves. We keep restricted blanks in the shop, we cut them by code or by decoding your existing key, and we walk you through the authorization step when a system requires one.

Why you got turned away

Four reasons the kiosk said no.

01

They cannot buy the blank

Patented keyways are distributed under control. A hardware store rack holds open blanks only, so there is physically nothing to cut your key onto.

02

The machine is not set up for it

Many high-security keys need angled cuts, side milling, or a specific jaw and cutter. A duplicator built for house keys will not reach those surfaces.

03

There is no authorization record

Restricted systems are tracked. Someone has to hold the key control card or be on the authorization list. A self-serve kiosk has no way to check that.

04

Nobody tests the result

A high-security key that is close is a key that jams in your cylinder. Ours get checked in a matching cylinder at the counter before you leave with them.

The key itself

Bring the original if you still have one, not a copy of a copy. Worn keys get decoded rather than traced so the new one matches the cylinder, not the wear.

Key control card or code

Issued with restricted systems, often kept by the owner or managing agent. If you have it, bring it. If it is lost, we will explain the route back.

Photo ID and proof of address

A lease, a utility bill, or a letter from management for commercial doors. We log restricted work, which is the whole point of a controlled system.

A photo of the lock

For upgrades and rekeys, snap the face of the cylinder and the edge of the door. It tells us the cylinder type and backset before you carry anything in.

Related work

More than a spare key.

Most people who come in for a high-security key end up asking about the lock behind it. We handle that side too, for apartments, brownstones, storefronts, and buildings with dozens of doors.

Upgrading to a high-security cylinder

Many deadbolts and mortise locks accept a replacement cylinder, so the lock body and the door stay as they are. You get a patented keyway and a fresh set of keys without new holes in the frame. We do the same on storefront doors and gates.

Lost high-security keys

No key left to copy is still a solvable problem. We originate keys by code where a record exists, decode the cylinder where it does not, and rekey the system if you want the missing key to stop working entirely.

Building and master systems

One key for the super, one for each tenant, one for the boiler room. We build and maintain master keyed systems on restricted keyways for co-ops, rentals, and commercial spaces, and keep the records so the next change is simple.

Questions at the counter

The five we hear every week.

If yours is not here, call the shop. A locksmith picks up, not a call center.

Do I need the key control card? +

For some restricted systems, yes. The card or authorization code is issued with the system and names who is allowed to order keys. Bring it if you have it, along with photo ID. If you are not sure what your system requires, read the stamping on the key to us over the phone and we will tell you before you make the trip.

Can you copy a key stamped "Do Not Duplicate"? +

That stamp is a request from whoever issued the key rather than a legal restriction, and it offers no real protection on an open keyway. We ask for proof that you own the door, are the tenant of record, or are authorized by the managing agent. If the key also sits on a patented restricted keyway, the system's own authorization rules apply on top of that.

How long does it take? +

Most high-security keys are cut and tested at the counter while you wait. Less common keyways sometimes need a blank ordered in, and restricted systems may need the authorization confirmed first. Call ahead and we will tell you whether it is a walk-in job or a come-back job.

Can you upgrade my existing deadbolt to high-security? +

Usually. A lot of deadbolts and mortise locks take a replacement cylinder, so the lock body stays on the door and only the cylinder and keys change. Bring the lock in if it is loose, or send us a photo of the face and the edge of the door and we will tell you which cylinders fit it.

What if I lost all my keys? +

Bring the cylinder to the shop if you can remove it, or we come to the door. We originate keys by code when a record exists and decode the cylinder when it does not. On a restricted system we confirm your authorization first. If you want the lost keys dead, we rekey the cylinder at the same visit.

5 Ways CCTV Can Improve the Security of Your House

A camera at the door changes behavior before anything else happens. It discourages the casual package thief, it records who came and went, and it gives you something concrete to hand the police or your insurer. Add remote viewing and you can check the front door from work, and pair it with a solid deadbolt so the hardware backs up the picture. We cover all five, including the mistake of aiming a camera at a spot no one actually walks through.

Reasons Why It Is Important to Install CCTV

Cameras do three jobs a lock cannot: they deter, they document, and they settle disputes. For a building entrance or a shop floor, footage answers the question of what happened at 2:14am without anyone guessing. We explain what a camera realistically prevents and what it only records.

Best Places to Install CCTV Cameras in Your House

Front door first, then the back or service entrance, then the hallway everyone has to cross. Ground floor windows and the mailroom come next. Height matters as much as angle: too high and you record the top of a hat, too low and the lens gets covered.

How Much Does It Cost to Install CCTV?

The camera is rarely the expensive part. Cable runs, mounting surfaces, storage and the number of viewing points drive the number. We break the estimate into pieces so you can see what a one-door setup costs versus a full building, and where people overspend.
